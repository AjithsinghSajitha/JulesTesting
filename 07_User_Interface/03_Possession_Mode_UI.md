# UI Mockup: Possession Mode (First-Person View)

The UI in Possession Mode is designed with one primary goal: **maximum immersion**. The player should feel like they are seeing the world through the character's eyes, not looking at a video game screen. Therefore, the UI is minimalist, contextual, and relies heavily on diegetic (in-world) cues rather than a traditional HUD.

## 1. The Default View: An Uncluttered Screen

By default, the screen is almost completely free of UI elements. The player sees only the game world from the first-person perspective. There is no persistent health bar, no mini-map, and no quest log.

*   **Interaction Reticle:** A single, small, semi-transparent dot in the center of the screen. This is the only persistent UI element. When the player looks at an interactive object or NPC, the dot will subtly highlight, and a single word prompt will appear (e.g., "Talk," "Open," "Take").

## 2. Diegetic and Contextual Status Indicators

Information about the character's status is communicated through in-world audio and visual cues, not bars and meters.

*   **Health/Damage:**
    *   When the character takes damage, the screen will flash red at the edges.
    *   As health gets lower, the screen will become progressively desaturated, and the character's breathing will become ragged and audible.
    *   Heavy damage will cause a blood splatter effect on the screen, which slowly fades.
*   **Stamina:**
    *   Sprinting or performing heavy actions will cause the character's breathing to become heavier.
    *   If stamina is fully depleted, the player will hear a "heart pounding" sound effect, and their vision will blur slightly at the edges.
*   **Needs (Hunger/Thirst):**
    *   When a character becomes hungry, the player will hear a subtle stomach growl sound effect. The character's inner monologue (their "voice") might make a comment like, "I could really go for some bread."
    *   When thirsty, the character's breathing will sound slightly drier, and their inner monologue might mention needing a drink.

## 3. The "Inner Monologue" as a Quest Log

There is no traditional quest log. Instead, the character's goals and current "quests" (Opportunities) are tracked via their inner monologue.

*   **Accessing Goals:** By holding a "Focus" key (e.g., 'Q'), the player can quiet the outside world slightly and hear the character's current thoughts. The inner monologue voice will summarize their current priorities.
    *   Example: "The mayor wants me to deal with those wolves... but I promised my wife I'd fix the fence today. And I'm getting hungry."
*   This system feels organic and grounds the player in the character's immediate concerns.

## 4. The Inventory and Character Screen

The inventory and character menus are designed to feel like physical objects within the world.

*   **Access:** Pressing the 'Tab' key does not bring up a full-screen menu. Instead, the character performs an animation of opening their satchel or bag, and the camera looks down at the contents.
*   **Layout:** The "menu" is a representation of the character's bag. Items are represented as 2D icons laid out on a leather or cloth texture. The player can see their equipped items on a paper-doll style representation of their character.
*   **Stats and Skills:** Tabs at the top of this view, looking like stitched leather bookmarks, allow the player to switch to a "Journal" view. This is where they can see their character's skills and attributes, presented as if written in a personal journal.

## 5. Compass and Navigation

There is no persistent mini-map. Navigation is intended to be done by landmarks.

*   **Contextual Compass:** If the player needs directional guidance, they can press a key to have their character hold up a physical compass. This appears as a 3D object in the character's hand at the bottom of the screen, which the player can reference before it is put away again.

By stripping away the traditional UI, Possession Mode forces the player to engage with the world directly, listen to its sounds, and pay attention to their character's needs, creating a deeply immersive and rewarding experience.
