# Procedural Generation: Quests and Events

To create a truly dynamic and replayable experience, "Dynasty of the Voiceless" moves away from a traditional, static quest system. Instead, quests (referred to as "Opportunities") and world events are generated procedurally by the "World AI" in response to the simulated state of the world. This ensures that the player is always reacting to the real, current needs of the world's inhabitants.

## 1. The Opportunity System

Instead of a list of pre-written quests, the game uses a system that identifies "problems" or "needs" within the simulation and flags them as Opportunities for NPCs (and the player) to solve.

*   **How it Works:** The World AI constantly scans the state of NPCs, settlements, and the environment. When it finds a significant imbalance, it creates an Opportunity.
*   **The Structure of an Opportunity:**
    *   **Problem:** The core issue (e.g., "The village of Oakwood has a food shortage," "A pack of wolves is menacing the northern trade route," "The blacksmith has run out of iron ore").
    *   **Goal:** The desired outcome (e.g., "Bring 50 units of food to Oakwood," "Kill the alpha wolf," "Deliver 20 units of iron ore to the blacksmith").
    *   **Actors:** The key NPCs involved (e.g., the village mayor, the trade caravan leader, the blacksmith).
    *   **Reward:** The payment or reputation gain for solving the problem. This is calculated dynamically based on the urgency and difficulty.

*   **Player Discovery:** The player discovers these Opportunities organically by talking to people or observing the world. They might hear the mayor complaining about the food shortage in the tavern, or see a "Help Wanted" notice on the town board.

## 2. Examples of Emergent Opportunities

*   **Simple Needs:**
    *   **Problem:** A farmer's fence is broken. **Opportunity:** Gather wood and repair the fence for a small fee.
    *   **Problem:** The local alchemist needs a rare herb. **Opportunity:** Venture into the dangerous forest to find it for a handsome reward.
*   **Complex Chains:** Opportunities can link together to create emergent storylines.
    1.  **Initial Problem:** A bandit camp is established near a mining village.
    2.  **Opportunity 1:** The village mayor asks for someone to clear out the bandits.
    3.  **If Ignored:** The bandits raid a trade caravan, causing a...
    4.  **New Problem:** ...shortage of tools in the village.
    5.  **Opportunity 2:** The blacksmith now needs a delivery of iron to craft new tools.
    6.  **And so on...** A small problem, if left unsolved, can spiral into a major crisis for a settlement.

## 3. Procedural World Events

Beyond small-scale Opportunities, the World AI can trigger large-scale events that can affect entire regions or factions. These are not scripted; they are the result of accumulated pressures in the simulation.

*   **Triggers:**
    *   **Economic:** A long-term trade imbalance between two kingdoms, a major resource discovery (like a gold rush), or a widespread famine.
    *   **Political:** A king dying without a clear heir, a faction growing too powerful, or a long-standing border dispute reaching a boiling point.
    *   **Environmental:** An unusually harsh winter, a spreading plague, or a monster migrating into civilized lands.

*   **Examples of World Events:**
    *   **The King's Tournament:** A prosperous and stable kingdom might host a grand tournament, creating many Opportunities for knights, merchants, and even spies.
    *   **The Peasant Uprising:** A kingdom with high taxes and widespread famine might experience a peasant rebellion. The player could choose to join the rebels, help the king suppress them, or simply profit from the chaos.
    *   **The Wyvern's Fury:** A Wyvern, displaced from its mountain lair by a harsh winter, might begin attacking villages in the lowlands. This creates a high-stakes Opportunity for a group of heroes to band together and hunt the beast.
    *   **War:** The ultimate world event. If diplomatic tensions between two factions reach a breaking point, they will declare war. This triggers a cascade of military-themed Opportunities: recruitment drives, supply runs, scouting missions, and epic battles.

## 4. Player Agency

The player is a key part of this system. Their actions (or inaction) can directly cause or resolve these events.

*   **Starting a War:** By possessing a king and fabricating a diplomatic incident, the player could plunge the entire continent into war.
*   **Preventing a Famine:** By possessing a farmer and showing them how to use irrigation during a drought, the player could save a region from starvation.

This procedural system ensures that the world of Veridian is never static. It is a living, breathing place full of problems to solve, crises to avert, and stories to be made.
