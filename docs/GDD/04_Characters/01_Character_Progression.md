# Character Progression

In "Dynasty of the Voiceless," character progression is not based on abstract experience points (XP) or rigid class structures. Instead, characters improve through a dynamic, organic system of "learning by doing." This applies to every single NPC in the world, whether the player is possessing them or not.

## 1. Core Philosophy: Skills Improve with Use

The fundamental principle is simple: the more a character performs an action, the better they get at it. A character who spends their days chopping wood will become a better woodcutter. A character who frequently engages in sword fights will become a more skilled warrior.

*   **Skill Trees:** Every character has a set of skill trees that represent their potential abilities. These are broad categories like:
    *   **Combat:** (e.g., Swords, Bows, Shields, Armor)
    *   **Crafting:** (e.g., Blacksmithing, Woodworking, Alchemy)
    *   **Social:** (e.g., Persuasion, Intimidation, Deception, Leadership)
    *   **Survival:** (e.g., Foraging, Hunting, Tracking)
    *   **Knowledge:** (e.g., Medicine, Engineering, History)
*   **Skill Gain:** When a character performs an action, they gain a small amount of progress in the relevant skill. Forging a simple iron dagger will slightly increase the "Blacksmithing" skill. Successfully persuading a merchant to lower a price will increase the "Persuasion" skill.
*   **No Level-Gating:** There are no arbitrary level requirements. A character's ability to perform an action is directly tied to their skill level. A novice blacksmith can only attempt to craft simple items, while a master can work with rare materials and create legendary artifacts.

## 2. Talents and Specializations

As a character's skill level in a particular area increases, they will unlock "Talent Points." These points can be spent to unlock specializations and passive bonuses, allowing a character to further define their abilities.

*   **Example: Sword Skill Tree**
    *   **Novice (Skill 1-20):** Basic attacks.
    *   **Apprentice (Skill 21-40):** Unlocks a Talent Point. Can be spent on "Improved Parry" (better defense) or "Power Attack" (stronger offense).
    *   **Adept (Skill 41-60):** Unlocks another Talent Point. Can be spent on "Duelist's Stance" (faster attacks with one-handed swords) or "Cleave" (an area-of-effect attack for two-handed swords).
    *   **Master (Skill 81-100):** Unlocks powerful, defining abilities, such as a "Killing Blow" execution move or the ability to disarm opponents.

## 3. Player and NPC Progression

*   **Player-Controlled Progression:** When the player possesses an NPC, they have direct control over which talents to select when a point is earned. This allows the player to shape the development of their current host.
*   **AI-Controlled Progression:** When an NPC is not possessed, the "World AI" will automatically spend their talent points based on their personality, profession, and life experiences.
    *   A city guard AI will likely invest their combat points in defensive talents like "Improved Parry" and "Shield Wall."
    *   A bandit AI might choose more aggressive talents like "Power Attack" and "Ambush."
    *   A farmer AI will likely never gain combat talent points, but might unlock talents in the "Survival" tree like "Expert Forager" or "Animal Husbandry."

## 4. Attributes and Traits

Beyond skills, characters are defined by a set of core attributes and personality traits.

*   **Attributes:** These are innate characteristics like Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, and Charisma. They are largely determined at birth (based on parentage) and change very little over a character's lifetime. They provide a baseline modifier to skill gain and effectiveness. A character with high Strength will learn combat skills slightly faster and hit harder.
*   **Traits:** These are personality markers (e.g., Brave, Cowardly, Ambitious, Honest, Greedy) that are procedurally generated. Traits influence an NPC's AI behavior and can grant minor bonuses or penalties. A "Brave" character might get a small combat bonus when outnumbered, while a "Greedy" character might be more effective at haggling prices. Traits can change over time based on significant life events.
