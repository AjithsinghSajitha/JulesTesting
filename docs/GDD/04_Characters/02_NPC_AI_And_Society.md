# NPC AI and Society: The "World AI"

The "World AI" is the invisible engine that drives the society of Veridian. It is a complex, multi-layered simulation designed to give every NPC a believable and persistent life. The goal is that every character you see, from a king to a beggar, is a unique individual with their own story, not just a static object waiting for player interaction.

## 1. The Hierarchy of Needs

At the core of every NPC's behavior is a system inspired by Maslow's hierarchy of needs. Every NPC is constantly trying to fulfill a set of needs, from the most basic to the most abstract.

*   **Physiological Needs (Highest Priority):** Hunger, thirst, and fatigue. An NPC's first priority is always survival. A starving NPC will drop everything to find food, whether by buying it, foraging for it, or stealing it.
*   **Safety Needs:** Shelter, security, and health. NPCs will seek to have a home. They will avoid dangerous areas and react to threats. If they are injured, they will seek healing.
*   **Social Needs:** Belonging, relationships, and family. NPCs desire social interaction. They will seek to make friends, find love, and build a family. They will feel "lonely" if isolated for too long.
*   **Esteem Needs:** Accomplishment, reputation, and respect. NPCs want to be good at their jobs and respected by their peers. A blacksmith will feel a sense of accomplishment from crafting a fine sword. A guard will feel pride in defending their town.
*   **Self-Actualization Needs (Lowest Priority):** Hobbies, creativity, and personal growth. In their spare time, NPCs will pursue hobbies. A farmer might enjoy fishing. A noble might try to write poetry. This is what characters do when all their other needs are met.

## 2. The Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP) System

To satisfy their needs, NPCs use a GOAP system. This means they don't follow rigid scripts. Instead, they look at their current state (e.g., "I am hungry"), and then formulate a plan to achieve a goal (e.g., "get food").

*   **Example: A Hungry Farmer**
    1.  **Need:** Hunger is high.
    2.  **Goal:** Obtain food.
    3.  **Possible Actions:**
        *   Go home and eat from storage. (Checks if there is food at home).
        *   Go to the tavern and buy a meal. (Checks if they have money).
        *   Go to the forest and forage for berries. (Checks the season and local environment).
        *   Go to a neighbor and ask for food. (Checks relationship with neighbor).
    4.  **Execution:** The NPC chooses the most logical and efficient action based on their current situation and personality. A wealthy farmer will go home; a poor one might have to forage.

## 3. The Social and Relationship Engine

Every NPC maintains a relationship value with every other NPC they have met. This value is influenced by every interaction.

*   **Relationship Modifiers:**
    *   **Positive:** Giving a gift, doing a favor, having a friendly conversation, agreeing in a debate.
    *   **Negative:** Insults, threats, theft, violence, disagreeing on important topics.
*   **Family Bonds:** Family members have a strong positive baseline relationship. This is the strongest bond in the game.
*   **Faction Allegiance:** Members of the same faction (e.g., the city guard) have a moderate positive baseline. Rival factions have a negative baseline.
*   **Reputation:** NPCs have a reputation that precedes them. If an NPC has a reputation for being a hero, others will start with a positive disposition towards them.

## 4. The "World Knowledge" Database

Every NPC has a limited "knowledge" of the world. They only know what they have personally seen, been told, or what is common knowledge in their community.

*   **Information Spread:** Information spreads organically. If a farmer sees a wolf near town, they will run to the town square and tell others. The guards who hear this will then gain the knowledge of the wolf's presence and may form a patrol to hunt it.
*   **Rumors and Gossip:** Not all information is accurate. NPCs can misremember details or intentionally spread false rumors to damage a rival's reputation. This can lead to emergent, unscripted events based on misinformation.
*   **The Player's Role:** The player, in Possession Mode, can be a primary vector for information. Telling a secret to a known gossip is a sure-fire way to spread it through a town.

This combination of needs, goals, relationships, and knowledge creates a complex and dynamic society where every NPC is an individual, and the world feels truly alive and reactive.
