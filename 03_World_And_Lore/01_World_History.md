# World History: A Chronicle of Veridian

The history of Veridian is a long and storied tapestry, marked by the rise and fall of empires, cataclysmic events, and the slow, inexorable march of civilization. The known history is typically divided into four great Ages.

## The First Age: The Age of Whispers (Ancient Past)

*   **A World Unwritten:** Little is known for certain about this primordial era. It is a time of myth and legend, spoken of only in the most fragmented of texts. It is said that the world was a raw, untamed wilderness, populated by powerful elemental spirits and strange, magical beasts.
*   **The First Men:** The ancestors of modern humans arrive in Veridian, scattered and tribal. They are simple hunter-gatherers, living in fear of the wild world.
*   **The Voice from the Void:** It is during this time that the entity known as the "Voiceless Spirit" (the player) is thought to have first manifested, a silent observer to the birth of consciousness. Legends speak of certain individuals who were "touched by a spirit," leading their tribes to great discoveries like fire and language. These were likely the first instances of possession.

## The Second Age: The Age of Giants (The Mythic Era)

*   **The Rise of the Giants:** A race of colossal, intelligent beings, now known only as the "Giants," dominated Veridian. They were masters of stone and earth, and the colossal ruins and monolithic structures that dot the landscape are remnants of their civilization. They were not hostile, but viewed the fledgling human tribes as little more than curious animals.
*   **The Gift of Knowledge:** The Giants, in their inscrutable wisdom, taught the First Men the basics of agriculture, construction, and metalworking. This elevated humanity from scattered tribes into nascent city-states.
*   **The Great Sundering:** The Age of Giants ended in a cataclysm known only as the "Great Sundering." No one knows what caused it—a war with the heavens, a magical plague, or perhaps a civil war amongst themselves. The Giants vanished from the world, leaving behind their mysterious ruins and a world forever changed.

## The Third Age: The Age of Heroes (The Imperial Era)

*   **The First Kingdom:** In the wake of the Giants' disappearance, humanity flourished. The first great human kingdom, the "Aethelian Empire," rose to prominence. It was founded by a legendary hero, Aethel the Great, who is said to have united the scattered city-states.
*   **A Golden Age:** The Aethelian Empire presided over a long golden age of peace and prosperity. Magic was studied and codified, great cities were built, and art and culture flourished. The Empire's legions kept the peace, and its influence spread across the entire continent.
*   **The Long Decline and The Shattering:** After a thousand years, the Empire grew corrupt and decadent. Ambitious governors and generals began to carve out their own fiefdoms. A series of brutal civil wars, known as the "Wars of Shattering," tore the Empire apart. The once-great monolith of civilization collapsed, leaving a power vacuum in its wake.

## The Fourth Age: The Age of Strife (The Present Day)

*   **A World of Factions:** The current age is defined by the aftermath of the Empire's fall. The old imperial provinces have become independent kingdoms, each with its own culture, ambitions, and bitter rivalries. Mercenary companies, religious cults, and powerful guilds thrive in the chaos, vying for influence and wealth.
*   **Lost Glories:** The knowledge and technology of the Age of Heroes have been partially lost. Magic is now rarer and more dangerous, and the great roads and aqueducts of the Empire have fallen into disrepair. Many look back on the Aethelian Empire as a lost paradise.
*   **An Age of Opportunity:** While a time of conflict, it is also a time of great opportunity. New dynasties can be forged, and old ones can be brought to ruin. In this fractured and dangerous world, a single person's actions—or the actions of the spirit possessing them—can change the course of history. This is the world the player enters.
